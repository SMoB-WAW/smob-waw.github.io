We watch single proteins work on single DNA molecules.

SMoB, the Single-Molecule Biophysics facility at the Institute of Biochemistry and Biophysics PAS in Warsaw, runs a correlative optical tweezers and confocal fluorescence microscope (C-Trap). We use it to study DNA replication and protein–DNA interactions, and we run measurements for collaborators.

The instrument

A single DNA molecule is held between two optically trapped beads. We control its tension, move it between laminar-flow channels containing different proteins, and image what binds to it with confocal scanning, while recording force at the same time.

Research

Replisomes at barriers

How a replisome encounters nucleosomes and tightly bound proteins: whether it stalls, displaces the barrier or bypasses it, and which factors decide the outcome.

Protein–DNA interactions

Binding, one-dimensional diffusion and compaction of DNA by single-stranded and double-stranded DNA-binding proteins, measured with force and fluorescence together.

Working with us

We collaborate with groups who need single-molecule measurements but do not have the instrument. Most projects follow the same path.

  1. Write to us with the biological question and the proteins involved.
  2. Feasibility. We discuss labelling, DNA constructs and the amount of purified protein needed.
  3. Pilot session. One or two days on the instrument to establish conditions.
  4. Measurements and analysis, carried out jointly, with data shared in full.
