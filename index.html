We hold and watch single molecules work.

SMoB, the Single-Molecule Biophysics Facility at the Institute of Biochemistry and Biophysics, Polish Academy of Sciences, in Warsaw, is equipped with a correlative optical tweezers and confocal fluorescence microscope (C-Trap) and a custom-built multiplexed magnetic tweezers setup. We use these technologies to investigate protein–DNA interactions, chromatin dynamics, transcription, and other molecular processes through collaborative research projects. Our facility also provides access to single-molecule measurements and expertise for external collaborators.

The instrument

Single-molecule tweezers enable the trapping and manipulation of individual biomolecules, such as DNA and proteins, through their attachment to microscopic beads. They allow precise application and measurement of forces, providing insights into molecular mechanics, dynamics, and interactions in real time.

Correlative otpical tweezers and confocal fluorescence microscopy (C-Trap)
Combines precise force manipulation of individual biomolecules with real-time fluorescence imaging to investigate molecular interactions, dynamics, and structural changes.
Multiplexed magnetic tweezers
A custom-built system enabling simultaneous manipulation and monitoring of multiple DNA molecules under controlled tension and torsion, ideal for studying DNA mechanics and protein–DNA interactions.

Research

Chromatin dynamics

We investigate the mechanical properties of chromatin, focusing on nucleosome unwrapping, higher-order chromatin structure, and its response to mechanical tension and torsion. Using single-molecule techniques, we explore how chromatin organization and dynamics influence DNA accessibility and function.

Protein–DNA interactions

Binding, one-dimensional diffusion and compaction of DNA by single-stranded and double-stranded DNA-binding proteins, measured with force and fluorescence together.

DNA supercoiling

We investigate how DNA topology and supercoiling influence fundamental biological processes, including transcription, DNA replication, and chromatin organization.

Working with us

We collaborate with groups who need single-molecule measurements but do not have the instrument. Most projects follow the same path.

  1. Write to us with the biological question and the proteins involved.
  2. Feasibility. We discuss labelling, DNA constructs and the amount of purified protein needed.
  3. Pilot session. One or two days on the instrument to establish conditions.
  4. Measurements and analysis, carried out jointly, with data shared in full.
